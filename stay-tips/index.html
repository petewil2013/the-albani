Tips for your stay

A few things we've learned from living here. None of this is mandatory — it just makes a Maine stay smoother. If anything here is unclear when you arrive, text the on-call number we sent in your confirmation.

Bug season — be ready

Central Maine in summer is beautiful, and it is buggy. Ticks, mosquitoes, black flies, and deer flies all do their work here. A little preparation makes the difference between a great evening on the deck and an itchy one indoors.

Bring (or ask us)

  • DEET (30% or higher) or picaridin insect repellent for skin. Apply before you head out, reapply after swimming or heavy sweat.
  • A Thermacell portable bug deterrent and refill cartridges. It creates a roughly 15-foot bubble of protection — set it on the deck or beside the fire pit and bugs largely leave you alone. There is usually one in the house; bring spare butane and mats if you want certainty.
  • Permethrin-treated shirts, pants, and socks if you plan to hike. Treatment lasts ~6 washes; safe once dry.
  • Long pants tucked into socks for trail walks. It looks goofy. It works.
  • Fine-tipped tweezers (kept in the bathroom drawer here too, but a personal pair is handy).

Tick checks — do them daily

  • End each day with a full-body check. Pay attention to the hairline, behind ears, neck, armpits, waistband, groin, and behind knees.
  • If you find an attached tick, grasp it as close to the skin as possible with fine tweezers and pull straight up with steady pressure. No match heads, no Vaseline.
  • Save the tick in a sealed bag with the date for ~30 days. If a bullseye rash, flu-like symptoms, or joint pain appear later, you have the tick to show a clinician.
  • The closest urgent care and emergency services are listed below under Emergency & medical.

Around the house

  • Keep screen doors closed. They look closed when they aren't.
  • Check shoes and bags before they come inside, especially after walking through tall grass or brush.
  • The Thermacell goes outside only — it is not for indoor use.

A cool, comfortable stay in summer

The house cools beautifully if you treat it like a thermal battery. The strategy is simple: let the cold in at night, hold it in during the day.

The routine

  • Evening (sunset onward): open the windows wide. Maine summer nights typically drop into the 50s or low 60s — pull that cool air through the whole house.
  • Morning (before it gets hot, usually by 8–9 AM): close the windows and draw the blinds along the rear of the house. The rear face takes the strongest afternoon sun, and the blinds make a measurable difference.
  • Afternoon: resist the urge to open up. The interior will stay noticeably cooler than outside if you hold the line.
  • Evening again: as soon as the outside drops below the inside, reverse — open windows, lift blinds.

If you forget for an afternoon, no harm done; the next overnight cycle will reset the house. The pattern matters more than the perfection.
